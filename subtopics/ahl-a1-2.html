Structure

A1.2.11

Direction in DNA and RNA

pp. 25–26

Phosphodiester bonds link carbon 3′ of one sugar to carbon 5′ of the next, so each strand has a 5′ end (phosphate) and a 3′ end (free -OH). The two strands of DNA are antiparallel. New nucleotides join only the 3′ end, so DNA and RNA are always built 5′ to 3′. In replication, the two strands are copied in opposite directions; in transcription, RNA polymerase reads the template strand 3′ to 5′; in translation, ribosomes read mRNA from 5′ to 3′.

A1.2.12

Purine-pyrimidine pairs

pp. 26–27

Purines (adenine, guanine) have two rings; pyrimidines (cytosine, thymine) have one. Each base pair is one of each, so A-T and C-G pairs are the same width and the helix keeps the same shape whatever the sequence. Hydrogen bonds (two in A-T, three in C-G) hold the pairs together.

A1.2.13

Nucleosomes

pp. 27–29

In eukaryotes, DNA is wound about twice around a core of eight histone proteins. Linker DNA runs to the next nucleosome, and an H1 histone holds the DNA in place. Positively charged lysine and arginine in histones attract the negative phosphates of DNA. Packing fits about 2 m of DNA into each nucleus, lets chromosomes condense for division, and helps control which genes can be transcribed.

Evidence

A1.2.14

Hershey and Chase

pp. 29–30

In 1952, phages were labelled with 35S (in protein) or 32P (in DNA) and allowed to infect E. coli. Blending removed the phage coats and centrifuging separated them from the cells. Most 32P went into the bacteria and some appeared in new phages; most 35S stayed outside. So DNA, not protein, carries the genetic information.

A1.2.15

Chargaff’s data

pp. 31–32

Chargaff measured base proportions in DNA from many species. In every species A = T and G = C, so purines equal pyrimidines; but the proportions differ between species. This falsified the tetranucleotide hypothesis, which said DNA was a simple repeat of the four bases and could not carry information, and it supported complementary base pairing.

Nature of science and TOK

Nature of science

Experiments

Avery's team pointed to DNA as the genetic material in 1944, but many scientists were only convinced after Hershey and Chase's 1952 experiment.

Theory of knowledge

Why is some evidence believed more readily than other evidence?
