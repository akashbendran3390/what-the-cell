Be the bird

A population of 60 moths rests on tree bark. Wing colour is controlled by one gene: the allele for dark wings (D) is dominant over the allele for pale wings (d). You are a bird: catch moths for a few seconds, then the survivors breed and the next generation appears. Watch how the allele frequencies change.

Based on the peppered moth: in polluted areas soot darkened tree bark and dark moths became common; when the air was cleaned up, pale moths became common again.

Try it

  1. Hunt on pale bark for five generations. Which moths are easiest to catch? What happens to the frequency of allele d?
  2. Switch to sooty bark and keep going. Describe and explain the change.
  3. Start again with the dark allele at 2%. How long before dark moths are common on sooty bark? Why is it slow at first?
  4. Set predation to zero. Do the frequencies still change? (This is what happens without selection.)
