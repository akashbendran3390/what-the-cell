Forces and Safety

What forces are and how we measure them, why mass and weight are different, how ramps, friction, air resistance and floating work, what balanced and unbalanced forces do, how to calculate speed, and how helmets and seat belts keep us safe.

MYP 1, Unit 412 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: BalanceRelated: MovementRelated: EvidenceGlobal context: Identities and relationships

Statement of inquiryUnderstanding how forces change movement helps us stay safe in sport and in daily life.

Factual
What types of force are there? What is the difference between mass and weight? How is average speed calculated?
Conceptual
How do balanced and unbalanced forces change the way objects move? How does safety equipment reduce the force on the body in a collision?
Debatable
Should helmets be compulsory for cyclists and scooter riders? Why do so many people think a moving object needs a force to keep going?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • describe forces as pushes or pulls measured in newtons, contact or non-contact
  • distinguish mass and weight and calculate weight with W = m × 10
  • explain how a ramp reduces the force needed and how slope changes speed
  • describe friction and explain when it is useful or a nuisance
  • plan and carry out a fair test on friction
  • explain air resistance and how shape changes it
  • explain floating and sinking with weight and upthrust
  • describe how charged objects and magnets attract and repel
  • draw force arrows and calculate a resultant force
  • calculate average speed and read a distance-time graph
  • explain how helmets, seat belts and crumple zones reduce injuries

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Forces Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
