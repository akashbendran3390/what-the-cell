Lesson 10: Bond energies

Notes: Bond energies

Breaking and making

Breaking a bond always takes in energy (endothermic). Making a bond always gives energy out (exothermic). The bond energy is the energy needed to break one mole of a bond, in kJ/mol.

The equation

ΔH = energy to break bonds − energy released making bonds. Negative ΔH: exothermic. Positive: endothermic.

Bond values used

H–H 436, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 431, O=O 498, O–H 464, C–H 414, C=O 803, C–C 347, N–H 389, triple N 946 kJ/mol.

Hydrogen chloride

H2 + Cl2 → 2HCl. Broken: 436 + 243 = 679. Made: 2 × 431 = 862. ΔH = 679 − 862 = −183 kJ/mol.

Burning methane

CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O. Broken: 4 C–H + 2 O=O = 2652. Made: 2 C=O + 4 O–H = 3462. ΔH = −810 kJ/mol. Fuels release energy because the new bonds are stronger overall.

Why not exact

Bond energies are averages from many molecules, and they are for gases. The measured value for methane is −890 kJ/mol because the water forms as a liquid.
