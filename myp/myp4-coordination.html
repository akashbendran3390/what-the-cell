Coordinated Body Systems

How your body senses the world and responds: senses, nerves and the brain for fast messages, hormones for slower ones, the feedback loops that keep you steady, and how plants respond without any nerves at all.

MYP 4, Unit 514 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: SystemsRelated: Form and functionRelated: MovementRelated: BalanceGlobal context: Identities and relationships

Statement of inquiryCoordinated responses depend on organisation and communication between body systems.

Factual
How does the nervous system carry messages? What is a reflex action? How do hormones control change? How do plants respond to light and gravity?
Conceptual
How do the nervous and endocrine systems communicate and work together? How does negative feedback keep conditions steady?
Debatable
Is there such a thing as a normal body? Should people use drugs such as caffeine to change how their nervous system works?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • write the stimulus-to-response pathway and compare nerves with hormones
  • label the eye and the ear and explain the pupil reflex
  • describe sensory, relay and motor neurones and how they are adapted
  • explain how a message crosses a synapse and how caffeine and alcohol affect it
  • draw a reflex arc and explain why reflexes are fast
  • plan a fair reaction time investigation and analyse its data
  • give the jobs of the cerebrum, cerebellum and brain stem
  • name the main glands, their hormones and jobs
  • explain negative feedback for temperature, blood glucose and water
  • compare type 1 and type 2 diabetes
  • describe the effects of adrenaline
  • explain phototropism with auxin and give uses of plant hormones

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Coordination Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
