Is my topic good enough?

Answer honestly. One “no” means talk to your teacher before Task 1.

Also ask yourself: Can I keep at least three other variables constant? Will living material grow or arrive in time? What is my back-up if the organism fails? Is there a twist that makes this question mine rather than a copy of a classic practical?

Which statistical test?

Significance level in biology: p < 0.05. A test only works on the kind of data it was made for: chi-squared needs raw counts, never percentages or means.

Uncertainties for biology equipment

Equipment or method Typical uncertainty Notes
Analogue scale (ruler, measuring cylinder, thermometer) ± half the smallest division A 10 cm³ cylinder marked every 0.2 cm³ gives ± 0.1 cm³
Digital readout (balance, probe, colorimeter) ± 1 in the last digit A balance reading 2.46 g gives ± 0.01 g
Graduated pipette or syringe ± half the smallest division Read at eye level from the bottom of the meniscus
Hand timing with a stopwatch about ± 0.2 s Reaction time is far bigger than the stopwatch’s ± 0.01 s
Counting (colonies, stomata, bubbles, organisms) Difference between two counts of the same sample Recount a few samples, or ask a partner to count blind
Quadrat percentage cover Difference between two observers Use a gridded quadrat to make estimates more consistent
Database values The uncertainty the source states If none is given, say so in your evaluation

When you divide or multiply values, add their percentage uncertainties. For most biology IAs, the spread between trials (SD) is bigger than the instrument uncertainty: say which is bigger and why.

Cut these first: general background about the whole topic, repeating the method in the results, describing every row of a table, step-by-step software instructions, and apologies (“I may have…”).

Weak and creditworthy evaluation

Weak Creditworthy
“Human error affected the results.” “Potato cylinders were blotted by hand, so surface water varied; this gave the large SD at 0.4 mol dm⁻³ (± 1.8%). Rolling each cylinder twice on the same paper towel would standardise drying.”
“I should have done more trials.” “With five trials, one anomaly at 50 °C moved the mean by 14%. Ten trials per level would reduce its effect and narrow the error bars, making the optimum clearer.”
“The temperature was not controlled.” “The hot plate cycled between 25 and 35 °C, so respiration rate varied within each trial. A thermostatic water bath (± 0.5 °C) would hold the temperature steady.”

Use the chain: name the specific weakness → show its evidence in your data → say how much it matters and which way it pushed the results → give a realistic improvement and how it helps.

Before you hand in the complete draft

Pass all three gates before Task 7:

  1. Conclusion gate. My first sentence answers the research question with real values, and I compare my result with at least one cited published study.
  2. Uncertainty gate. Every raw data header has units and ± uncertainty, my error bars are defined in the caption, and I say how the spread affects my confidence.
  3. Evaluation gate. I have three design weaknesses with evidence from my own data, ranked by impact, each with a specific improvement.
