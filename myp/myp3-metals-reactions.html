Metals and Useful Reactions

Where metals sit on the periodic table, why their properties make them useful, which metals react fastest, how rust and electroplating work, and how we model chemical reactions with equations.

MYP 3, Unit 314 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: FormRelated: FunctionRelated: ModelsRelated: ConsequencesGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryScientific and technical innovations use the properties of metals and models of chemical change to predict and control useful reactions.

Factual
Where are metals, non-metals and metalloids on the periodic table? What is the reactivity series? How do we test for hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide?
Conceptual
How do the properties of a metal decide its use? How does the law of conservation of mass help us balance equations?
Debatable
Is it worth recycling every metal? Should rust-proofing be required for all steel?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • locate metals, non-metals and metalloids on the periodic table
  • describe the properties of metals and non-metals
  • describe alkali metals, alkaline earth metals and transition metals
  • define alloy and explain why alloys are useful
  • use the reactivity series to predict reactions
  • distinguish corrosion, rust and tarnish and describe rust prevention
  • describe how metals make electricity and how electroplating works
  • distinguish physical and chemical changes and list signs of a reaction
  • describe the tests for hydrogen, oxygen and carbon dioxide
  • write word and symbol equations and explain conservation of mass
  • balance symbol equations
  • classify synthesis, decomposition, displacement and combustion reactions
  • predict the products of acids with metals, bases and carbonates

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Metals and Reactions Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
