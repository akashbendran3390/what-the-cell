With linked genes, the dominant alleles of both genes are on the same chromosome in a parent that is heterozygous for both (they stay together unless crossing over separates them).

Key idea

With two unlinked genes, a parent that is heterozygous for both makes four kinds of gamete in equal numbers, because the chromosomes line up independently in meiosis. Crossing two such parents gives a 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 phenotype ratio. If the genes are linked, the parental combinations of alleles stay together, so the results move away from this ratio. The chi-squared test tells you whether a difference from the expected ratio is too big to be due to chance.

Clues to look for

  1. Two unaffected parents with an affected child: the allele must be recessive (both parents are carriers).
  2. An affected child always has at least one affected parent, and two affected parents can have an unaffected child: dominant.
  3. Mostly males affected, passed from unaffected mothers to sons, never from father to son: sex-linked recessive.
