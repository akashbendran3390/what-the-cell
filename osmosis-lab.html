Key ideas

  1. Osmosis is the net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane, from a less concentrated solution (more free water) to a more concentrated solution (less free water).
  2. Water molecules always move both ways at random. Osmosis is the difference between the two flows.
  3. Solute molecules attract water molecules (hydrogen bonds), so fewer water molecules are free to move on the side with more solute.
  4. When both sides have the same solute concentration (isotonic), the two flows balance and there is no net movement.

Each tube holds a potato cylinder in a different sucrose solution. Press Soak to see what happens.

How the practical is done (and a worked example)
  1. Cut cylinders of potato the same diameter with a cork borer and trim them to the same length.
  2. Blot each one dry and weigh it (initial mass).
  3. Put one in each sucrose solution (0.0 to 1.0 mol dm-3) for 24 hours at the same temperature.
  4. Take them out, blot dry the same way and weigh again (final mass).
  5. Work out percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.

Worked example: a cylinder of 2.50 g that ends at 2.85 g changed by (2.85 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = +14%. Percentage change lets you compare cylinders that started at different masses.

Try it

  1. Run the practical. Which cylinders gained mass, and which lost it? Explain using osmosis.
  2. Read the concentration where the line crosses zero. What does this tell you about the potato cells?
  3. Add repeats. Why does the class use percentage change and several repeats?
  4. HL: use the solute potential of the isotonic solution to estimate the water potential of the potato tissue.

What is happening

In dilute solutions the potato cells contain more solute than the solution, so water enters by osmosis and the cylinders gain mass. In concentrated solutions water leaves and they lose mass. Where there is no change, the solution and the cell contents are isotonic.

Water moves from higher to lower water potential. Adding solute lowers water potential, so pure water (ψ = 0 kPa) has the highest possible value.
