Genetics

How features pass from parents to children: DNA, genes and chromosomes, Mendel and Punnett squares, blood groups and sex linkage, family trees, mutations and the genetic technology that is changing medicine and farming.

MYP 5, Unit 414 lessonsCriteria A and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: PatternsRelated: TransformationGlobal context: Identities and relationships

Statement of inquiryPatterns in how genes are passed on and changed help us understand our relationships with our families and with other living things, and the choices that science now offers us.

Factual
What are DNA, genes and chromosomes? How are features passed from parents to children? What causes mutations?
Conceptual
How does the order of bases decide a feature? Why are some conditions more common in males? How can a family tree show how a condition is inherited?
Debatable
Should we change the genes of crops, animals or people? Who should have access to a person's DNA?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • link nucleus, chromosome, DNA and gene
  • describe the double helix and base pairing
  • explain how a gene codes for a protein
  • classify variation and choose the right graph
  • use allele, dominant, recessive, genotype and phenotype
  • draw a full genetic diagram in five lines
  • use a test cross and work back from ratios
  • explain incomplete dominance, codominance and ABO blood groups
  • explain XX, XY and X-linked inheritance
  • read a pedigree and spot the pattern
  • describe mutations, mutagens and four genetic conditions
  • explain DNA profiling, GM and gene therapy, with a benefit and a concern

Revise in this order

  1. Work through the revision deck below: try each quick check before you click on.
  2. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  3. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then check it with your teacher.
  4. Practise crosses and pedigrees in the Genetics Lab.
