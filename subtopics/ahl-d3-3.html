The kidney

D3.3.7

Excretion and osmoregulation

pp. 768–770

Excretion removes waste products of metabolism, such as urea. Osmoregulation controls the osmotic concentration of body fluids, measured in osmol L−1. The kidney does both: it leaves wastes in the urine and varies how much water it reabsorbs.

D3.3.8

Ultrafiltration and reabsorption

pp. 771–772

High pressure in the glomerulus forces plasma through fenestrated capillaries, the basement membrane and slits between podocytes into Bowman’s capsule. Cells and proteins stay behind. In the proximal convoluted tubule, cells with microvilli and many mitochondria reabsorb all the glucose, amino acids and most salt and water, leaving urea and toxins in the filtrate.

D3.3.9

Loop of Henle

pp. 773–774

The ascending limb actively pumps Na+ into the medulla but does not let water out, so the medulla becomes very concentrated. This lets water be drawn out of the collecting ducts later.

D3.3.10

ADH and the collecting duct

pp. 775–776

Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect concentrated blood, and the pituitary secretes more ADH. ADH causes vesicles carrying aquaporins to fuse with the membrane of collecting duct cells, so more water is reabsorbed. When blood is dilute, ADH falls and aquaporins are taken back into vesicles.

Blood supply

D3.3.11

Blood supply and activity

pp. 777–778

Arterioles dilate or constrict to send blood where it is needed. In vigorous exercise flow to skeletal muscle rises about twentyfold while flow to the gut and kidneys falls; brain flow stays nearly constant. In sleep, flow to muscle falls.

Practical

Modelling filtration in the kidney

Model40 minD3.3.8

You will need: Dialysis tubing, mix of starch and glucose, water, beaker, iodine solution, glucose test strips

  1. Fill knotted tubing with the mix, to represent blood in the glomerulus
  2. Stand it in water for 15 minutes
  3. Test the water for each substance
  4. Compare this model with ultrafiltration and identify what it leaves out

Safety: Eye protection when using iodine. Wipe up spills.

Carry this out with your teacher's supervision and follow your school's lab rules.

Nature of science

Global impact of science
