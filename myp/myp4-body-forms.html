Body Forms

How a body of 37 trillion cells works as one: cells and their parts, how substances get in and out, digestion, breathing, the heart and blood, and how waste is removed.

MYP 4, Unit 214 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: TransformationRelated: FormRelated: FunctionRelated: ScaleGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryComplex organisms need many interactive systems to work together.

Factual
How are complex organisms organised? What are the advantages of specialisation? Can an organ belong to more than one system?
Conceptual
How do cells respond to change? What can structure teach us about function? Where can technology improve health?
Debatable
Is form more important than function? How much of our body can technology replace?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • label animal and plant cells and give the job of each organelle
  • calculate magnification and describe how to use a microscope
  • describe adaptations of specialised cells and put cell, tissue, organ, system, organism in order
  • define diffusion and osmosis and explain what changes the rate
  • predict what happens to animal and plant cells in different solutions
  • calculate percentage change in mass and name the variables in an investigation
  • compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport
  • calculate SA:V and explain why large organisms need exchange surfaces
  • name the organs of the digestive system in order and their jobs
  • name amylase, protease and lipase, their substrates and products, and explain denaturing
  • explain how villi and alveoli are adapted
  • describe breathing in and out
  • trace blood through the heart and compare arteries, veins and capillaries
  • describe how the kidney filters blood and compare dialysis with a transplant

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Body Forms Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
