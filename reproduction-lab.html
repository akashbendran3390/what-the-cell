    How the strip works

    Add a urine sample to the left end of the strip.

    1. Mobile antibodies near the end of the strip bind to hCG. They carry a coloured dye.
    2. Urine carries them along the strip by capillary action.
    3. At the test line, fixed antibodies bind a different part of hCG, holding hCG plus its dye-carrying antibody: a coloured line.
    4. At the control line, fixed antibodies bind the mobile antibodies themselves, so a line appears whether or not hCG is present. It shows the test worked.

    hCG is made by the embryo (later the placenta) from about a week after fertilization. It keeps the corpus luteum making progesterone. Syllabus: D3.1.17 (HL)
