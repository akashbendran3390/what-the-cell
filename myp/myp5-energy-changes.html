MYP Science / MYP 5 / Unit 2 / Chemistry

Making use of energy changes in chemical reactions

Why some reactions warm their surroundings and others cool them, how to measure the energy stored in fuels, and how to calculate energy changes from bonds and from Hess’s law.

MYP 5, Unit 213 lessonsCriteria A, B and C

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: EnergyRelated: EnvironmentGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryOur understanding of chemical reactions and the energy changes that go with them has led to innovative new products in our lives.

Factual
What makes a reaction exothermic or endothermic? What is a bond energy? What does burning fossil fuels do to the environment?
Conceptual
How do bond energies let us predict the energy change of a reaction? How can we measure the energy released by a fuel accurately? Why does Hess’s law work?
Debatable
Do we really need products such as self-heating cans?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • tell heat from temperature
  • name and explain conduction, convection and radiation
  • use Q = m × c × ΔT, and work out a mixed temperature
  • calculate a percentage difference
  • sort changes into exothermic and endothermic
  • draw and label a reaction profile, with Ea and ΔH
  • explain hot and cold packs
  • write combustion equations; explain why carbon monoxide is dangerous
  • calculate kJ/g from a calorimeter experiment
  • calculate ΔH from bond energies
  • use Hess’s law: reverse, multiply, add

Revise in this order

  1. Work through the revision deck below: try each question before you click on.
  2. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and redo each worked example without looking.
  3. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then check it with your teacher.
  4. Practise in the Energy Changes Lab: every calculation tab checks your answers.
