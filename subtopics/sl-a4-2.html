Biodiversity, past and present

A4.2.1

Biodiversity in all its forms and levels

pp. 156–159

Biodiversity includes ecosystem diversity, species diversity and genetic diversity. Species diversity depends on richness, the number of species, and evenness, how similar their numbers are. Simpson's reciprocal index compares communities: D = N(N − 1) / Σn(n − 1), and a higher value means higher diversity.

A4.2.2

Today compared with the past

pp. 160–161

Millions of species have been named, and many more remain to be discovered. Fossils suggest that more species are alive now than at any time in the past, although past levels are hard to estimate.

A4.2.4

Causes of ecosystem loss

pp. 164–167

Causes include farming, logging, mining, dams, draining water, pollution and climate change. Mixed dipterocarp forest in South East Asia is being lost to timber and plantations such as oil palm. Your local example could be Swiss moorland or river floodplains.

A4.2.5

Evidence of a biodiversity crisis

p. 168

Reports from IPBES and other sources draw on surveys of many habitats and groups. They include population sizes, species ranges, richness and evenness, and how degraded ecosystems are. Citizen scientists can add data too.

A4.2.6

Causes of the current crisis

pp. 169–173

Human population growth is the overarching cause. Specific causes are hunting and over-exploitation, urbanization, deforestation and land clearance, pollution, and invasive species.

What can be done

A4.2.7

Why several approaches are needed

pp. 174–178

No single approach is enough. In situ methods include nature reserves, rewilding and reclaiming degraded land. Ex situ methods include zoos, botanic gardens and seed banks.

A4.2.8

The EDGE of Existence programme

pp. 178–179

EDGE gives priority to species that are Evolutionarily Distinct and Globally Endangered. With limited funds, saving a species with few close relatives protects more unique evolutionary history.

Practical

Comparing species in two habitats

Field50 minA4.2.1

You will need: Quadrats, identification guide or app, clipboard

  1. Choose two contrasting areas, such as mown and unmown grass
  2. Place five random quadrats in each
  3. Record the types of plant and how common each is
  4. Compare the two sites, and suggest reasons for any difference

Safety: Check the site first. Watch for stinging plants, wash hands afterwards and consider allergies.

Carry this out with your teacher's supervision and follow your school's lab rules.
