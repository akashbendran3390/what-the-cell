Ions and Our Health

From atoms to your dinner plate: how atoms become ions, how ions build compounds, how we write and balance equations, how to test for ions, and which ions keep your body healthy.

MYP 4, Unit 314 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: ModelsRelated: Cause and effectRelated: EvidenceGlobal context: Identities and relationships

Statement of inquiryScientists continue to seek better understandings of the role of various elements in human health.

Factual
What are atoms made of? How do atoms become ions? How can we test for ions? Which ions does the body need?
Conceptual
How do models of the atom help us explain bonding? How does the concentration of ions affect conductivity? How does the dose change whether a substance helps or harms?
Debatable
Should everyone take mineral supplements? Should governments add elements to food and water, such as iodine in salt?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • give the charge, mass and position of protons, neutrons and electrons
  • use atomic number and mass number to count particles
  • draw shell diagrams for the first 20 elements and link outer electrons to the group
  • predict the charge on an ion from the group number
  • describe ionic bonding and draw dot-and-cross diagrams
  • write formulae of ionic compounds, including polyatomic ions with brackets
  • name ionic compounds, including Roman numerals
  • write word and symbol equations with state symbols
  • balance equations and use conservation of mass
  • explain the melting point, conductivity and brittleness of ionic compounds
  • describe flame tests and precipitate tests for ions
  • plan the conductivity investigation and analyse its data
  • give the jobs of calcium, iron, iodine, sodium and potassium and the effects of too little or too much
  • explain hard water, how to soften it, and evaluate claims about supplements

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Ions Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
