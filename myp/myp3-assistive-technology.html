Assistive Technology

What disability means, how technology helps people take part in daily life, and the physics of the simple machines behind ramps, levers, pulleys, gears and hydraulic lifts.

MYP 3, Unit 514 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: SystemsRelated: EnergyRelated: FormRelated: FunctionRelated: EquityGlobal context: Fairness and development

Statement of inquiryWith appropriate design, and attention to form and function, technology can be used to ensure equal opportunities for people with disability.

Factual
What is a disability? What are the five simple machines? How is the efficiency of a machine calculated?
Conceptual
How do simple machines make work easier? How can design give people equal opportunities?
Debatable
Should all public buildings be fully accessible by law? Who should pay for assistive technology?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • explain what disability means and the difference between body structure, function and taking part
  • describe assistive technologies such as prosthetic limbs, hearing aids, sip-and-puff switches and GNSS
  • calculate work done and explain that machines do not reduce work
  • calculate the force needed on an inclined plane
  • identify the three classes of lever
  • use the principle of moments to calculate forces and distances
  • explain how a wheel and axle and a screw help
  • calculate mechanical advantage with pulleys
  • calculate gear ratios and predict turning speed
  • explain how bicycle gears change force and speed
  • calculate efficiency
  • explain how hydraulic systems multiply force
  • design an assistive device using simple machines

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Machines Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
