Counting, growing, eating and being eaten

A population is all the members of one species in an area. Estimate the size of a population you cannot count, watch a population grow to its carrying capacity, run predators and prey together, sort the relationships between species, and test whether two species are found together more than chance would predict.

Kinds of interaction

Competition: both lose. Predation and herbivory: one eats the other. Parasitism: one benefits, the host is harmed. Pathogenicity: a pathogen causes disease. Mutualism: both benefit. Within a species, members both compete and cooperate.
