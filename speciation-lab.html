Speciation Lab

DP Biology A4.1 · a model, not real data

Split a population and watch new species form

A population of finch-like birds lives across a valley. The west side is dry with small hard seeds; the east side is wet with large seeds. Each bird has a beak size (shown by its size) and a courtship song (shown by its colour). Birds only mate with partners whose song is similar enough.

Raise a river to split the population, run many generations, then remove the river. Can the two groups still interbreed?

Generation 0

Try it

  1. Run 20 generations with no river. Do the two sides become different? Why not?
  2. Raise the river and run about 100 generations. Describe how beak size and song change on each side, and explain why.
  3. Remove the river. Watch "matings that work between the groups". Have the groups become separate species? What kind of reproductive isolation is this?
  4. HL: start again with no river and press "Polyploid mutation". Run 20 generations. Explain why the polyploid birds form a new species at once, without any barrier.
