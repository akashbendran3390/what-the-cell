Step by step

  1. Pain receptors (free nerve endings) in the skin are stimulated by heat.
  2. An impulse travels along the sensory neuron through the dorsal root to the spinal cord.
  3. In the grey matter, the sensory neuron passes the signal across a synapse to a relay neuron.
  4. The relay neuron passes it to a motor neuron, which leaves through the ventral root.
  5. The effector, a skeletal muscle (biceps), contracts and pulls the hand away.
  6. Meanwhile another relay neuron carries the signal up the spinal cord to the brain, where the pain is felt – after the hand has already moved.

What is happening?

Heart rate-Breathing rate-Breath depth (tidal volume)-Ventilation-Blood CO2 / pH-Blood pressure-

Chemoreceptors in the aorta, carotid arteries and medulla detect CO2 and pH. Baroreceptors in the aorta and carotid arteries detect blood pressure. The medulla sends signals along sympathetic nerves (speed up) or the vagus nerve (slow down) to the heart's pacemaker, and along nerves to the diaphragm and intercostal muscles.

Syllabus: C3.1.12, C3.1.14, C3.1.15

Try these

  1. Go from resting to sprinting. Which rises first, heart rate or blood CO2? Why do both rise?
  2. Hold your breath. What triggers the strong urge to breathe again?
  3. Stand up quickly. Which receptors detect the change, and how does the heart respond?
  4. Give a sudden fright. Which gland releases adrenaline, and what does it prepare the body for?
  5. Explain why these are negative feedback loops.

What is happening?

Day-Where you are-Body clock out of step by-Melatonin starts at (local time)-

Light reaching special cells in the retina signals to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus. In darkness the SCN lets the pineal gland secrete melatonin, which makes you sleepy. Light, especially blue light, stops melatonin secretion.
