Flight

How people learnt to fly: forces, density and pressure, buoyancy and floating, balloons and airships, wings and lift, helicopters, momentum and rockets, and the impact of flight on our world.

MYP 5, Unit 615 lessonsCriterion A

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: DevelopmentRelated: FormRelated: FunctionGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryUnderstanding the forces and pressures on objects in fluids has driven the development of flight.

Factual
What force must every flying thing overcome? What are air pressure and water pressure? Which forces make lift?
Conceptual
How does buoyancy explain balloons and submarines? How does the shape of a wing make lift? How does momentum explain rockets?
Debatable
Has war driven the development of flight? Should long-haul flights be limited to protect the climate?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • tell mass from weight and use W = mg
  • draw free-body diagrams and find resultant forces
  • calculate density and predict floating
  • calculate pressure with P = F/A and explain hydraulics
  • use P = ρgh and explain atmospheric pressure
  • state Archimedes’ principle and calculate upthrust and apparent weight
  • explain ships, submarines and icebergs
  • calculate the forces on a balloon
  • describe the four forces and Bernoulli’s principle
  • explain lift, the angle of attack and stalling
  • explain why helicopters need a tail rotor
  • use conservation of momentum for collisions and rockets
  • evaluate the impact of flight on society and the environment

Revise in this order

  1. Work through the revision deck below: try each quick check before you click on.
  2. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  3. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then check it with your teacher.
  4. Practise in the Flight Lab.
