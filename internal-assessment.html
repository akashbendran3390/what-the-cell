Marks 4 criteria × 6 marks = 24, worth 20% of your final Biology grade
Time About 10 hours of investigation, spread over a 16-week route
Length Up to 3,000 words. Tables, graphs, captions, equations, calculations, headings, citations, references and the AI declaration are not counted

Where the marks are. Half the marks (Conclusion and Evaluation) come from the last two sections, which students often rush. Plan your time so that Week 8 is protected for writing them.

The four criteria in plain words

Criterion What the examiner wants to see Where you build it
Research design /6 A focused question set in its biology; choices that give enough relevant data, each one explained; a method someone else could repeat Stages 1–5
Data analysis /6 Clear, precise tables and graphs; uncertainty taken seriously; the right processing and statistics, done accurately Stages 6–9
Conclusion /6 An answer to your question that your analysis fully supports, explained with biology and compared with published work Stage 10
Evaluation /6 Specific weaknesses in your design, how much each one matters, and realistic improvements that would fix them Stage 11

Moving up a band almost always means moving from stating (1–2) to describing (3–4) to explaining or justifying (5–6). The top band always needs a reason, not just a description.

The golden rules

  • Start one working document on Day 1 and keep everything in it: notes, sources, photos, raw data and drafts.
  • Data collection takes two to three times longer than you expect. Living organisms take longer still, so start plants, cultures and seeds first.
  • Never write proves. Data supports or suggests.
  • Write I, never we or our: this is an individual report.
  • Write scientific names in italics: Escherichia coli in full the first time, then E. coli.
  • Cite every source and declare every use of AI as you go.
  • If you are stuck at any stage, ask your teacher straight away.

How feedback works. Tasks 1–6 get short comments so you can keep moving. Your teacher gives full written feedback once, on the complete draft (Task 7), within 3 weeks. You then have 4 weeks from the day you receive it to revise. There is no second full review, so make Task 7 your best work.

Academic integrity

  • Your own work. You may collect data with classmates, but your question, analysis and writing must be your own. Say in your report which data was shared.
  • Cite as you go. Every source, image, protocol, video and calculator you used goes in your works cited, with the URL and date accessed.
  • Declare AI. Name the tool, what you used it for, the prompts, and how you used what it gave you. Undeclared AI use is treated as academic misconduct.
  • Stay anonymous. No names (including yours), school name, photos of faces or exact locations in the report.
  • Ethics first. No harm to vertebrates; human participants give informed consent (parental consent under 16); microorganisms follow the school’s microbiology rules; fieldwork leaves the site as you found it.
