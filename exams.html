Exams and command terms

How the two Biology papers are built at SL and HL, how to prepare for each part, and what every command term asks you to do.

Final grade Paper 1 is 36%, Paper 2 is 44% and the internal assessment (IA) is 20%
Same structure SL and HL sit the same two papers. HL papers are longer and include AHL content
Calculators Allowed in both papers
Penalties No marks are taken off for a wrong multiple-choice answer, so answer every question

These facts come from the IB Biology guide (first assessment 2025). Check exam dates and rules with your school’s DP coordinator.

The two papers

SL HL
Paper 1 total 1 h 30 min, 55 marks 2 h, 75 marks
Paper 1A: multiple choice 30 questions (30 marks) 40 questions (40 marks)
Paper 1B: data-based 4 questions linked to practical work (25 marks) 4 questions linked to practical work (35 marks)
Paper 2 total 1 h 30 min, 50 marks 2 h 30 min, 80 marks
Section A A data-based question and short-answer questions (34 marks) A data-based question and short-answer questions (48 marks)
Section B Answer one of two extended-response questions (16 marks) Answer two of three extended-response questions (32 marks)

Paper 1A and 1B are one sitting. They come as two booklets but are taken together without a break, so plan your time across both: about one minute per multiple-choice question leaves enough time for the data questions.

How to prepare for each part

  • Paper 1A (multiple choice). Read all four options before choosing. Cross out the ones you know are wrong. Practise with timed sets of 10.
  • Paper 1B (data-based). These questions are built around experiments. Use the Skills page and the data-based questions to practise reading graphs, error bars and tables, and quote numbers with units in your answers.
  • Paper 2, Section A. Mark totals tell you how many points to make: a 3-mark answer needs three separate, correct points.
  • Paper 2, Section B. Plan for two minutes before writing. Use diagrams where they help, define key terms, and finish each part before moving to the next.
  • The nature of science. Questions can ask how scientists know something, so learn the example on each subtopic page and on the Nature of Science page.

Command terms

Every exam question starts with a command term. It tells you how much to write. The definitions below are in our own words; the official glossary is in the IB Biology guide.

Level 1: know it (short answers)

Term What to do Biology example
Define Give the exact meaning of a term Define osmosis.
Draw Make an accurate, labelled diagram or graph. Use a ruler for straight lines and plot points carefully Draw a labelled diagram of a mitochondrion.
Label Add names to the parts of a diagram Label the chambers of the heart.
List Write a series of short answers, no explanation List three products of the light-dependent reactions.
Measure Find a value, for example with a ruler or scale bar Measure the length of the cell in the micrograph.
State Give a name, value or short answer, no explanation State the function of ribosomes.

Level 2: apply it (describe and work out)

Term What to do Biology example
Annotate Add short notes to a diagram or graph Annotate the graph to show the lag phase.
Calculate Work out a number and show your working Calculate the magnification of the image.
Describe Give a detailed account of what happens or what the data shows Describe the trend in the graph.
Distinguish Make clear how two things differ Distinguish between DNA and RNA.
Estimate Find an approximate value Estimate the population size from the quadrat data.
Identify Pick the answer from several possibilities Identify the stage of mitosis shown.
Outline Give a short account or summary Outline the process of transcription.

Level 3: think with it (explain, judge, create)

Term What to do Biology example
Analyse Break something down to show its key parts or patterns Analyse the results of the experiment.
Comment Give a judgement on a statement or result Comment on the reliability of the data.
Compare Give the similarities, referring to both things throughout Compare mitosis and meiosis.
Compare and contrast Give the similarities and the differences, referring to both throughout Compare and contrast the structure of arteries and veins.
Construct Present information as a diagram, table or logical sequence Construct a food web from the information.
Deduce Reach a conclusion from the information given Deduce the genotype of the parents.
Design Produce a plan, model or simulation Design an experiment to test the effect of pH on catalase.
Determine Find the only possible answer Determine the number of chromosomes in a gamete.
Discuss Give a balanced review with arguments on more than one side, and a conclusion backed by evidence Discuss the use of stem cells in medicine.
Evaluate Judge something by weighing its strengths against its limitations Evaluate the method used in the investigation.
Explain Give a detailed account with reasons or causes Explain how the structure of alveoli suits gas exchange.
Justify Give valid reasons or evidence for an answer or conclusion Justify your choice of statistical test.
Predict Give the result you would expect Predict the effect of a higher temperature on the rate.
Sketch Draw a rough diagram or graph that shows the general shape and key features Sketch a graph of enzyme activity against temperature.
Suggest Put forward a possible answer, reason or hypothesis Suggest a reason for the anomalous result.

The most common mistake: describing when the question says explain. Describe says what happens; explain says why. If the question says explain, every point needs a reason, often with “because” or “so”.
