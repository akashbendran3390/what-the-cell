DP Biology D1.3 · real gene sequence for haemoglobin; the evolution model uses invented sequences

Changing the code

A gene mutation is a change to the base sequence of a gene. In this lab you edit a real gene and see what happens to the protein, follow random mutations into body cells and gametes, and (HL) use CRISPR-Cas9 to knock a gene out or edit it precisely, then find out why some genes have hardly changed in a billion years.

Edit the gene with CRISPR-Cas9

This cell carries the sickle cell allele (GTG in codon 7). Knock the gene out, or correct it.

A 20-base guide RNA (purple) must match the target, and the target must be followed by a PAM sequence NGG (gold), which Cas9 needs in order to bind. Click a G that is part of a GG pair to choose a PAM site.

Syllabus: D1.3.8, D1.3.9

Try these

  1. Find a PAM site in the first half of the gene and knock the gene out. Why does a small insertion or deletion stop the protein working?
  2. Repeat, but repair with a template that corrects the sickle cell base. Why is this harder for a cell than just joining the ends?
  3. Scientists knock out a gene in mice and the mice cannot see colour. What does this tell them?
