Flight Lab

Balance the forces on balloons, planes, helicopters and rockets, measure density and pressure, test Archimedes’ principle, float a boat and a submarine, launch a balloon, stall a wing and crash carts to test momentum.

Choose a flying machine and set the forces. The lab draws the free-body diagram with arrows to scale and finds the resultant force. Balanced forces mean no change in motion.

Choose a material and a cube size. Read the mass and volume, calculate the density, then drop the cube into a liquid. Objects less dense than the liquid float.

Test Archimedes’ principle with a newton meter, load a boat to its Plimsoll line, or flood a submarine’s ballast tanks.

Choose the gas, the size and the load. The lab finds the upthrust (weight of air displaced, air = 1.29 kg/m3), the total weight including the gas, and the resultant force.

A light aircraft (1200 kg, wing area 16 m2) on the runway. Change the air speed and the angle of attack: faster air over the top means lower pressure above. Lift must beat weight to fly; too big an angle and the wing stalls.

Crash two carts and check that momentum (p = mv) is conserved, or fire balls out of a rocket in space and watch it speed up.

Rocket 1000 kg without balls, carrying five 100 kg balls. Each ball leaves at 200 m/s relative to the rocket. Start at rest in space.
