Notes: Charge

Inside an atom

Protons (positive) and neutrons (no charge) are in the nucleus; electrons (negative) are around it. A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons, so the charges cancel.

Charging by rubbing

Rubbing moves electrons from one material to the other. The object that gains electrons becomes negative; the one that loses them becomes positive. Protons do not move.

Forces between charges

Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. A charged object also attracts a neutral one, because it pulls or pushes the electrons inside it. The closer the charges, the bigger the force.

Charge is conserved

Charge is never made or destroyed, only moved. When a balloon and cloth are rubbed, the balloon gains exactly the charge the cloth loses, so the total stays the same.

The coulomb

Charge is measured in coulombs (C). One electron has a charge of minus 1.6 × 10−19 C, so 1 C is the charge of about 6.25 × 1018 electrons. Charge = number of electrons × 1.6 × 10−19 C.

Sparks and earthing

When enough charge builds up it can jump through air as a spark; lightning is a giant spark. Sparks can ignite fuel, so tankers and pumps are earthed to let charge flow away safely.

chargedhaving more or fewer electrons than protons

coulomb (C)the unit of charge; one electron has minus 1.6 × 10−19 C
repel / attractpush apart (like charges) / pull together (unlike charges)
