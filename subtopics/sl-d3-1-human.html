Why reproduce sexually?

D3.1.1

How sexual and asexual reproduction differ

pp. 693–694
AsexualSexual
ParentsOneUsually two
GametesNoneHaploid gametes fuse
OffspringGenetically identicalNew combinations of genes
Relative advantageSuits individuals already adapted to an existing environmentProvides the variation needed to adapt to a changed environment
ExamplesYeast, hydra, aphids in summerMost animals, including humans

Some organisms do both. Aphids reproduce asexually when conditions are stable in spring and summer, and sexually in autumn.

D3.1.2

What meiosis and the fusion of gametes do in a sexual life cycle

pp. 694–695

Meiosis halves the chromosome number to make haploid gametes. Fertilization, the fusion of gametes, restores the diploid number. Without meiosis, the number would double every generation.

These two events also create variation. Meiosis breaks up parental combinations of alleles, and random fusion of gametes produces new combinations, unique to the new individual.

Watch out: 23 and 46 apply to humans only. Define a haploid cell as having one set of chromosomes.

D3.1.3

How male and female sexes differ in sexual reproduction

p. 695

The prime difference is that the male gamete travels to the female gamete. So it is small, with few food reserves. Few reach the egg, so very large numbers must be made.

The female gamete does not travel. It is larger, with food reserves for the early embryo, and far fewer are made.

In many animal species, this difference leads to different reproductive strategies: males compete and can mate many times, while females invest more in each offspring and may be more selective. These are broad patterns across species, with many exceptions.

D3.1.5

What happens in the ovarian and uterine cycles, and how hormones control them

pp. 698–700

The menstrual cycle lasts about 28 days and combines an ovarian cycle, preparing and releasing an egg, and a uterine cycle, building up and shedding the endometrium.

  1. Menstruation marks day 1. The pituitary gland secretes FSH, which stimulates follicles to develop.
  2. A follicle secretes oestradiol, which rebuilds the endometrium. It also increases FSH receptors in the follicle, so more oestradiol is made: positive feedback.
  3. High oestradiol inhibits FSH, so no more follicles develop: negative feedback.
  4. High oestradiol also stimulates a surge of LH, which triggers ovulation at about day 14.
  5. LH converts the empty follicle into a corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone and some oestradiol.
  6. Progesterone maintains the endometrium and inhibits FSH and LH: negative feedback.
  7. Low LH and FSH let the corpus luteum break down. Progesterone and oestradiol fall, and the endometrium is shed.

If an embryo implants, it secretes a hormone that keeps the corpus luteum going, so progesterone stays high.

Watch out: FSH stimulates follicle growth, while LH triggers ovulation. When progesterone falls and FSH rises, that is the removal of negative feedback and not positive feedback.

Fertilization and IVF

D3.1.6

How fertilization happens in humans

p. 701

Fertilization is internal, usually in the upper part of the oviduct.

  1. The cell membranes of the sperm and egg fuse.
  2. The sperm nucleus enters the egg. The tail and mitochondria of the sperm are destroyed, so all of the new cell's mitochondria come from the egg.
  3. The nuclear membranes of both nuclei break down.
  4. The condensed chromosomes from both nuclei take part in a joint mitosis, producing two diploid nuclei.

Watch out: avoid saying that the two nuclei fuse into one.

D3.1.7

How hormones are used in in vitro fertilization (IVF)

pp. 701–702

IVF is fertilization outside the body. Normal secretion of hormones is first suspended, so that doctors control the timing.

  • Down-regulation: a drug, often a nasal spray, stops pituitary and ovarian hormones for about two weeks.
  • Superovulation: high doses of FSH make many follicles develop together.
  • Maturation: an injection of hCG brings the eggs to maturity. They are collected about 36 hours later.
  • Fertilization and transfer: eggs are mixed with sperm. The embryos are incubated, and one or more are placed in the uterus.
  • Progesterone is given to maintain the endometrium.

Not every treatment leads to a birth, and transferring several embryos raises the risk of a multiple pregnancy.
