Solids, Liquids and Gases

What makes a solid, a liquid or a gas, how the particle model explains their behaviour, what happens when things melt, boil, evaporate and condense, why materials expand when heated, how smells spread, and how air pressure shapes our weather.

MYP 1, Unit 312 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: ModelsRelated: TransformationRelated: EnergyGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryThe particle model helps us explain and predict how matter changes when it is heated, cooled or squashed.

Factual
What are the properties of solids, liquids and gases? What are melting, boiling, evaporation, condensation and sublimation? What causes gas pressure?
Conceptual
How does the particle model explain changes of state? Why do materials expand when they are heated?
Debatable
Is a model ever completely true? How would melting, boiling and weather be different on Mars?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • compare the properties of solids, liquids and gases
  • use the particle model to explain the properties of each state
  • explain why gases can be squashed but solids and liquids hardly can
  • describe melting and freezing and read a heating curve
  • distinguish boiling from evaporation and explain why sweating cools you
  • plan a fair test on how clothes dry
  • describe condensation and sublimation with examples
  • explain how clouds, rain and fog form
  • explain expansion with the particle model and give uses and problems
  • explain diffusion and why it is faster when hot
  • explain gas pressure and link high and low pressure to weather

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Particle Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
