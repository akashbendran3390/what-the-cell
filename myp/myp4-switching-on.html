Switching On

Where our energy comes from and what it costs: forms of energy and efficiency, power and electricity bills, heat and particles, power stations, motors and generators, and the climate consequences of burning fossil fuels.

MYP 4, Unit 114 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: ConsequencesRelated: EnvironmentRelated: PatternsGlobal context: Globalisation and sustainability

Statement of inquiryOur present reliance on fossil fuels for electricity production is environmentally unsustainable on a global scale.

Factual
How do we measure electrical energy? What are the ways of generating electricity?
Conceptual
How is the enhanced greenhouse effect changing the Earth? What are the consequences of each way of generating electricity? How are electricity and magnetism connected?
Debatable
Why do many governments find it hard to act on climate change? Are our hopes for renewable energy too high?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • name the forms of energy and say whether each is moving or stored
  • write energy chains and state the law of conservation of energy
  • calculate efficiency and draw a Sankey diagram
  • use P = V × I and E = P × t, and change kW to W and minutes to seconds
  • work out kWh and an electricity bill from meter readings
  • describe particles in solids, liquids and gases, and tell heat from temperature
  • explain expansion and contraction with particles
  • explain conduction, convection and radiation, and how a vacuum flask works
  • sort energy sources into renewable and non-renewable, with an advantage and disadvantage of each
  • put the parts of a power station in order and explain why the grid uses high voltage
  • describe how to make an electromagnet stronger and how a motor turns
  • explain electromagnetic induction and how a generator works
  • explain the greenhouse effect and its consequences for Vietnam
  • weigh up the alternatives to fossil fuels and check a claim for misinformation

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Switching On Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Learn the formulas: efficiency = useful out ÷ total in × 100%; P = V × I; E = P × t; cost = kWh × price.
  5. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
