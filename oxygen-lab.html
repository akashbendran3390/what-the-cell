Picking up oxygen, letting it go

Haemoglobin has to do two opposite jobs: load oxygen in the lungs and unload it in the tissues. Move a red blood cell from the lungs to a resting or exercising muscle and watch the four haem groups fill and empty. Then add carbon dioxide (the Bohr shift) and compare adult with foetal haemoglobin at the placenta.
