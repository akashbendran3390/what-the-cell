B2.2.1

Organelles are discrete subunits with particular jobs

pp. 244–245

An organelle is a separate part of a cell, adapted to carry out one or more functions. Nuclei, vesicles, ribosomes and the plasma membrane all count.

MembranesExamples
TwoNucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast
OneVesicle, vacuole, lysosome, ER, Golgi apparatus
NoneRibosome

Watch out: the cell wall, cytoskeleton and cytoplasm are not organelles.

B2.2.2

Why the nucleus is kept separate from the cytoplasm

pp. 246–247

In eukaryotes, transcription happens in the nucleus and translation in the cytoplasm. mRNA can therefore be modified before it meets ribosomes. Prokaryotes have no nuclear membrane, and so ribosomes may start on mRNA as soon as it forms.

B2.2.3

Advantages of compartments in the cytoplasm

p. 247

Compartments concentrate enzymes and substrates, and separate processes that would interfere with one another. They also allow each region to keep its own conditions.

Lysosomes hold digestive enzymes behind a membrane at a low pH. The enzymes work only at this low pH, so any that leak into the neutral cytoplasm do little harm. Phagocytic vacuoles enclose engulfed food or pathogens, so that digestion takes place apart from the cytoplasm.

Practical

Observing chloroplasts in living cells

Lab30 minB2.2.1, B2.2.3

You will need: Elodea or moss leaf, slides, coverslips, microscope

  1. Mount one young leaf in a drop of water
  2. Find a single layer of cells at high power
  3. Draw the cell, showing chloroplasts
  4. Suggest why it helps to keep photosynthesis in separate organelles

Safety: Low risk. Take care with glass slides.

Carry this out with your teacher's supervision and follow your school's lab rules.

Nature of science

Evidence

Theory of knowledge

Why is it stronger when independent methods agree?

Seeing a structure and isolating its function are different kinds of evidence. Consider why agreement between methods increases confidence.
