MYP Science / MYP 5 / Unit 5 / Chemistry

Useful organic compounds

Where our fuels and plastics come from: crude oil and its fractions, alkanes, alkenes and isomers, cracking, addition and condensation polymers, and the big question of plastics and sustainability.

MYP 5, Unit 514 lessonsCriteria A and C

The big picture

Key concept: ChangeRelated: TransformationRelated: ConsequencesGlobal context: Globalization and sustainability

Statement of inquiryOur ability to develop new organic chemicals has resulted in many useful products, but increasingly questions are being raised about sustainability and stewardship of the environment.

Factual
What are hydrocarbons and where do they come from? How is crude oil separated? What are alkanes, alkenes and polymers?
Conceptual
How does the structure of a molecule decide its properties? Why can alkenes do reactions that alkanes cannot? How do polymers form?
Debatable
Should single-use plastics be banned? Should we stop using crude oil?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • define hydrocarbon and explain why carbon forms so many compounds
  • describe fractional distillation and name fractions and uses
  • link chain length to boiling point, viscosity and flammability
  • use the general formulas of alkanes, alkenes and alkynes
  • name and draw hydrocarbons and their isomers
  • balance combustion equations and describe substitution
  • explain cracking and write its equations
  • describe addition reactions and the bromine water test
  • explain saturated and unsaturated fats and hydrogenation
  • draw repeat units of addition polymers
  • compare addition and condensation polymers
  • evaluate plastics, recycling and sustainability

Revise in this order

  1. Work through the revision deck below: try each quick check before you click on.
  2. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  3. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then check it with your teacher.
  4. Practise in the Organic Molecules Lab.
