Becoming specialized

B2.3.1

From unspecialized cells to specialized cells

pp. 256–257

After fertilization, repeated division forms unspecialized, genetically identical cells. These then differentiate, as different genes are expressed in each cell type. In an early embryo, gradients of signalling chemicals called morphogens show a cell its position and so affect gene expression.

B2.3.2

Properties of stem cells

p. 257

Stem cells can divide endlessly, and can differentiate along different pathways.

B2.3.3

Stem cell niches in the adult body

pp. 258–259

A niche is a location that can maintain stem cells, or can promote their division and differentiation. Bone marrow produces all types of blood cell, and hair follicles produce new hair and help to repair skin.

Size and shape

B2.3.5

Cell size as part of specialization

pp. 260–262

Red blood cells are small enough to pass through capillaries. Eggs are large because they store food, whereas sperm are small and able to swim. White blood cells are larger than red ones, while neurons and striated muscle fibres can be very long.

B2.3.6

Surface area-to-volume ratio limits cell size

pp. 262–264

Exchange across the surface depends on area, whereas the need for exchange depends on volume. As a cell grows, volume rises faster than area, so the ratio falls. Materials then cannot enter or leave quickly enough, and the cell must stop growing or divide.

Practical

Surface area, volume and diffusion

Lab40 minB2.3.6

You will need: Agar cubes of three sizes containing a pH indicator, dilute hydrochloric acid, ruler, timer

  1. Calculate the surface area-to-volume ratio of each cube
  2. Place the cubes in dilute acid
  3. Time how long each takes to change colour all the way through
  4. Plot time against ratio, and relate this to limits on cell size

Safety: Eye protection. Use acid no stronger than 0.1 M.

Carry this out with your teacher's supervision and follow your school's lab rules.
