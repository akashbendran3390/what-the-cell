Motion and Car Safety

From a walk to school to a crash test: how we describe and graph motion, how forces change it through Newton's three laws, and how engineers use these laws to keep people safe on the road.

MYP 4, Unit 414 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: DevelopmentRelated: ConsequencesRelated: MovementGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryOur understanding of Newton's laws of motion has led to features that improve road safety.

Factual
What is the difference between distance and displacement? How do we calculate acceleration? What are Newton's three laws? What makes up a stopping distance?
Conceptual
How do graphs help us describe motion? How do forces change the way objects move? How do safety features reduce the force on people in a collision?
Debatable
Why are cars not made even safer? Should speed limits be lower in cities?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • use SI units and prefixes, and convert km/h to m/s
  • distinguish distance from displacement and scalars from vectors
  • calculate speed, and explain average and instantaneous speed
  • draw and read distance-time graphs (gradient = speed)
  • calculate acceleration with a = (v - u) / t
  • read velocity-time graphs: gradient = acceleration, area = distance
  • use v = u + at, s = (u + v)/2 x t and v2 = u2 + 2as
  • explain terminal velocity with forces
  • draw free-body diagrams and find the resultant force
  • state and apply Newton's three laws, including W = mg and F = ma
  • calculate thinking distance and explain what affects stopping distance
  • calculate momentum and explain how crumple zones, airbags, seat belts and helmets work

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Motion Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
