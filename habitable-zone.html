Habitable Zone Lab

DP Biology A1.1 (HL) · a simple model: real planets are more complicated

Could this planet have liquid water?

Life as we know it needs liquid water, so the search for life on other worlds starts by looking for planets that could hold it. Choose a star, move a planet nearer or further, change its size and atmosphere, and see whether its water would be ice, liquid or steam.

Try a real planet:

Try it

  1. With the Sun, find the nearest and furthest distances where Earth-like water stays liquid. That band is the habitable zone.
  2. Switch to a red dwarf. Where is the zone now, and why?
  3. Load Mars. It is close to the outer edge of the zone, yet has no liquid water on its surface today. Use its mass to explain why. What would need to change?
  4. Load Venus. Explain why being in or near the zone is not enough.
  5. Make the planet tiny (0.1 Earth masses). What happens to its atmosphere and water? Explain using gravity.
