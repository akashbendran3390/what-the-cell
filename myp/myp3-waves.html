Waves, Light and Sound

How waves carry energy, how we make and hear sound, why some sounds are high or loud, how fast sound travels, how mirrors and lenses control light, how the eye sees and how electromagnetic waves let us communicate.

MYP 3, Unit 414 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: CommunicationRelated: EnergyRelated: DevelopmentRelated: ConsequencesGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryDevelopments in technology are expanding our forms of communication, often with unknown consequences for our own lives.

Factual
What is a wave? How does the ear detect sound? What are the parts of the electromagnetic spectrum?
Conceptual
How do frequency and amplitude change what we hear? How do mirrors and lenses form images?
Debatable
Is modern communication technology making our lives better or worse? Should cities do more about noise?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • describe a wave and label wavelength and amplitude
  • compare transverse and longitudinal waves
  • explain how vibrations make sound and why sound needs a medium
  • describe the parts of the ear and their jobs
  • link loudness to amplitude and pitch to frequency, and read an oscilloscope trace
  • distinguish sound and noise and describe how materials absorb sound
  • calculate the speed of sound and distances from echoes
  • distinguish luminous and non-luminous objects and explain shadows
  • state the law of reflection and describe images in plane mirrors
  • explain how periscopes and kaleidoscopes work
  • describe refraction and the splitting of white light
  • describe the parts of the eye and their jobs
  • list the electromagnetic spectrum in order with a use of each

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Waves Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
