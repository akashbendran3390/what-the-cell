Stage 12: Compile the complete (100%) draft

Week 9 · Toddle Task 7 · Checkpoint 5 · Maximum 3,000 words

This is the draft that receives full written feedback, returned within 3 weeks. It must be complete and your best work. You cannot submit it until Tasks 1–6 are done.

Report structure

Title (reflects the research question)
Candidate code · Word count

  1. Research question
  2. Scientific context, theory and hypothesis
  3. Variables
  4. Methodology
  • Apparatus, materials and uncertainties
  • Justification of methods
  • Procedure (what you actually did)
  • Safety, ethical and environmental considerations
  1. Results
  • Raw data (or a representative sample) and control variable data
  • Qualitative observations
  1. Data analysis
  • Descriptive analysis with worked examples
  • Graphs and their description
  • Inferential analysis
  1. Conclusion
  2. Evaluation
  3. Works cited
  4. AI declaration

Before you submit

  • Check the thread: question, method, data, conclusion and evaluation refer to the same variables, organism, range and units.

  • Cut to the limit: remove general background and repetition. Tables, graphs, captions, equations, calculations, headings, in-text citations, references and the AI declaration do not count.

  • Write in paragraphs, not bullet points or question-and-answer form.

What each band looks like, in plain words

These are our own summaries to help you self-assess. Your teacher marks with the official IB descriptors.

Research design

Marks Question and context Planning for good data Method
1–2 The question is there, but the biology around it is missing You name some things that matter for the data Someone else could not repeat it
3–4 The question sits in a general area of biology You describe how you will get relevant data and enough of it Someone could repeat it, with a few gaps or guesses
5–6 The question sits in a focused, fitting biological context You explain why each choice gives relevant and sufficient data Someone could repeat it exactly

Data analysis

Marks Communication Uncertainty Processing
1–2 Hard to follow and imprecise Uncertainty barely considered Some useful processing, but big gaps or mistakes
3–4 Clear or precise, not both Uncertainty considered, with real gaps or mistakes Useful processing, with real gaps or mistakes
5–6 Clear and precise Uncertainty handled properly Useful processing, done properly and accurately

A big gap stops you reaching a valid conclusion. A real gap still lets you conclude, but limits how valid or detailed the conclusion is.

Conclusion

Marks Your conclusion Link to published science
1–2 Relevant, but your analysis does not back it up A surface-level comparison
3–4 Described, but does not fully match your analysis Some useful comparison
5–6 Justified and fully matches your analysis Justified by a relevant comparison

Evaluation

Marks Weaknesses Improvements
1–2 General weaknesses are named Realistic improvements are named
3–4 Specific weaknesses are described Realistic, relevant improvements are described
5–6 You explain how much each specific weakness matters You explain how each realistic improvement helps

Appendix B: Key terms

Term Meaning
State / Outline / Describe Brief answer / brief summary / detailed account (lower and middle bands)
Explain / Justify Detailed account with reasons or causes / valid reasons or evidence (top band)
Independent (manipulated) variable The variable you deliberately change
Dependent (responding) variable The variable you measure
Controlled variable Kept constant so it does not affect the result
Confounding variable Cannot be controlled; monitor and report it
Raw data Values read directly from the instrument
Uncertainty The range within which the true value lies (±)
Precision / Accuracy How close repeats are to each other / how close a value is to the true value
Random / Systematic error Unpredictable scatter (reduces precision) / consistent shift (reduces accuracy)
Validity / Reliability Measures what it claims to / gives the same result when repeated
Standard deviation (SD) Spread of values around the mean
Interquartile range (IQR) Spread of the middle half of the data
Skew How lopsided a distribution is compared with a normal curve
Correlation coefficient (r) Strength and direction of a relationship, −1 to +1
R² How well a trendline fits the data, 0 to 1
Null hypothesis (H₀) No significant difference, association or correlation
p-value The probability that results like yours occurred by chance
Significant p < 0.05
Percentage error |experimental − accepted| ÷ accepted × 100

Always check the details with your teacher: they follow the current IB Biology guide.
