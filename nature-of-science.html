Nature of Science

How biological knowledge is built and tested, with one real example for each subtopic.

SL path

Unit 1 Body Systems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
B3.1 (Animal)MeasurementsSpirometers turn breathing into numbers, but results vary with age, height, effort and the device, so a single reading needs careful interpretation.
B3.2 (Animal)EvidenceIn 1628 William Harvey argued that blood circulates, using measurements and reasoning. He could not see capillaries; Malpighi observed them decades later.
C2.2ExperimentsHodgkin and Huxley recorded nerve impulses from inside squid giant axons, because the large size made measurement possible.
C3.1ExperimentsIn 1902 Bayliss and Starling showed that a chemical, secretin, could trigger pancreatic secretion with nerves cut, revealing control by hormones.
D3.3Global impact of scienceIsolating insulin in 1921 turned type 1 diabetes from a fatal disease into a treatable one, saving millions of lives.
C3.2ObservationsIn the 1840s Ignaz Semmelweis noticed higher death rates where doctors came straight from autopsies, and found that handwashing reduced them.
D3.1 (Human)Global impact of scienceResearch on reproductive hormones led to contraception and, in 1978, the first baby born through IVF, changing many people's lives.

Unit 2 Plant Systems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
B3.1 (Plant)EvidenceResearchers found that stomatal density falls as carbon dioxide rises, so fossil leaves can be used to estimate past atmospheres.
B3.2 (Plant)HypothesesDixon and Joly proposed the cohesion-tension theory in 1894. It was hard to test directly, because inserting instruments disturbs the water under tension.
D3.1 (Plant)HypothesesDarwin saw an orchid with a very long nectar tube and predicted a moth with a matching tongue. It was described decades later.

Unit 3 Genetics

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
D1.1ExperimentsMeselson and Stahl used heavy nitrogen in 1958 to separate three models of replication, supporting the semi-conservative one.
D1.2ExperimentsNirenberg and Matthaei cracked the first codon in 1961, using a synthetic RNA made only of uracil.
D1.3ExperimentsIn 1943 Luria and Delbruck showed that bacterial mutations arise before selection, and are not a response to it.
D2.1ObservationsNew staining methods let Walther Flemming describe mitosis in 1882, showing how technique can open up what is visible.
D3.2Patterns and trendsMendel's large counts of pea plants revealed ratios that small samples would have hidden.

Unit 4 Cells

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A2.2ObservationsHooke and van Leeuwenhoek's microscopes revealed a world nobody had seen, and each improvement in microscopy has changed what biologists can ask.
B2.1FalsificationThe Davson-Danielli protein sandwich model was replaced by the Singer-Nicolson fluid mosaic model in 1972, as new evidence contradicted the older one.
B2.2EvidenceElectron microscopy and cell fractionation, developed by Claude, Palade and de Duve, together revealed organelles and what they do.
B2.3Global impact of scienceStem cell research promises new treatments. In 2006 Yamanaka's team reprogrammed adult cells, easing some objections to using embryos.
D2.3ExperimentsMeasuring mass change in tissue lets us infer water movement across membranes, even though we never see the molecules move.

Unit 5 Biomolecules

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A1.1FalsificationIn the late 1960s, scientists reported polywater, an unusual form of water. It was later traced to contamination.
A1.2ModelsWatson and Crick built their double helix model using Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data, which she had not given them directly.
B1.1EvidenceAdvice about saturated fats has shifted as studies improved, partly because diet is hard to measure and to control.
B1.2MeasurementsFrederick Sanger's sequencing of insulin in the 1950s showed that every protein has a precise order of amino acids.
C1.1ModelsFischer's lock-and-key model of 1894 was refined by Koshland's induced-fit model in 1958, as evidence showed that enzymes change shape.
C1.2ExperimentsIn the 1780s Lavoisier and Laplace compared the heat from a guinea pig with burning charcoal, linking respiration to combustion.
C1.3ExperimentsVan Helmont grew a willow in weighed soil and concluded that it gained its mass from water. His data were good, but he missed the role of air.

Unit 6 Biodiversity

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A3.1ObservationsFor decades, textbooks said that humans have 48 chromosomes. Better techniques let Tjio and Levan count 46 in 1956.
A4.1EvidenceEvolution is supported by independent evidence from fossils, anatomy, selective breeding and molecular sequences.
A4.2Global impact of scienceConservation science informs international policy, yet resources are limited, so scientists and governments must choose what to protect.
D4.1ObservationsPeter and Rosemary Grant measured Galapagos finches for decades, and directly observed beak size changing after droughts.
B4.2ExperimentsGause's experiments on Paramecium in 1934 showed that two similar competitors could not stably coexist, supporting competitive exclusion.

Unit 7 Ecosystems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
B4.1Patterns and trendsMapping climate against vegetation reveals biome patterns that help to explain where organisms live.
C4.1MeasurementsSampling lets us estimate populations that cannot be counted, with the assumptions and uncertainty stated.
C4.2MeasurementsCharles Keeling began measuring carbon dioxide at Mauna Loa in 1958. His careful, continuous record became a key piece of climate evidence.
D4.2ExperimentsIn 1966 Robert Paine removed a sea star from an area of shore and saw diversity fall, leading to the idea of keystone species.
D4.3Science as a shared endeavourArrhenius calculated the warming effect of carbon dioxide in 1896. Today the IPCC brings thousands of scientists together to assess the evidence.

AHL path

Unit H1 Cells

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A2.1ExperimentsIn 1953 Miller and Urey made amino acids from simple gases, showing that a step in life's origin was possible, though not that it happened that way.
A2.3EvidenceIn the 1890s Ivanovsky and Beijerinck found that a disease agent passed through filters that stopped bacteria, before anyone could see a virus.
A2.2EvidenceLynn Margulis's endosymbiosis theory faced strong resistance when published in 1967. Later genetic evidence strongly supported it.
B2.1ExperimentsIn 1970 Frye and Edidin fused mouse and human cells, and saw their membrane proteins mix, giving strong evidence that membranes are fluid.
B2.2ObservationsCamillo Golgi described his apparatus in 1898, but many thought it was a staining artefact until electron microscopes confirmed it.
B2.3ObservationsSome early microscopists believed they saw tiny humans inside sperm, an idea that shows how expectation can colour observation.

Unit H2 Biomolecules

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A1.1MeasurementsThe Rosetta mission measured the hydrogen isotopes in a comet's water, and found a ratio unlike Earth's, adding evidence to the debate on where our water came from.
A1.2ExperimentsAvery's team pointed to DNA as the genetic material in 1944, but many scientists were only convinced after Hershey and Chase's 1952 experiment.
B1.2ModelsAnfinsen's work suggested that sequence determines shape. In 2020 AlphaFold predicted many protein structures with high accuracy.
C1.1ModelsIn 1913 Leonor Michaelis and Maud Menten described enzyme kinetics with an equation that is still in use.
C1.2HypothesesPeter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory of 1961 was doubted by many. It later gained strong support, and he received a Nobel Prize in 1978.
C1.3ExperimentsMelvin Calvin's team traced radioactive carbon through algae to map the steps of carbon fixation.

Unit H3 Genetics

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
D2.2EvidenceStudies of people conceived during the Dutch Hunger Winter of 1944-45 linked famine to health effects decades later, with evidence of lasting epigenetic marks.
D1.1ExperimentsIn the 1960s, Reiji and Tsuneko Okazaki found short DNA fragments, which explained how a strand can be copied against its direction.
D1.2FalsificationIn 1977 Roberts and Sharp found that genes can be split. This overturned the simple one gene, one polypeptide picture.
D1.3Global impact of scienceDoudna and Charpentier developed CRISPR-Cas9 editing. In 2018 He Jiankui's announcement of edited babies drew worldwide condemnation.
D2.1Global impact of scienceCells taken from Henrietta Lacks in 1951, without her knowledge, became the HeLa line, which has supported countless discoveries about cell division.
D3.2Patterns and trendsMorgan's fruit fly results did not fit independent assortment. In 1913 his student Sturtevant used this to make the first genetic map.

Unit H4 Body Systems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
B3.3Science as a shared endeavourIn 1954 two independent teams, led by Andrew Huxley and Hugh Huxley, published the sliding filament idea together.
C2.1ExperimentsEarl Sutherland's work on epinephrine in the 1950s revealed second messengers, showing how a signal outside a cell can act inside it.
B3.1MeasurementsIn 1904 Bohr, Hasselbalch and Krogh measured how carbon dioxide alters haemoglobin's oxygen binding. Only one name stayed attached to the effect.
B3.2MeasurementsIn the early 1900s, Einthoven's string galvanometer recorded the heart's electrical activity, leading to the modern ECG.
C2.2ExperimentsIn 1921 Otto Loewi showed chemical transmission using two frog hearts. He later said that the idea came to him in a dream.
D3.3Global impact of scienceWillem Kolff built an early dialysis machine in the 1940s, and went on to save lives by applying physical principles to kidney failure.
D3.1EvidenceObservational studies suggested that hormone replacement therapy protected the heart. A large randomised trial reported in 2002 challenged this.

Unit H5 Plant Systems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
C3.1ExperimentsCharles Darwin and his son Francis covered shoot tips to study bending towards light. In 1928 Frits Went isolated the signal, later named auxin.
D2.3MeasurementsWater potential cannot be seen directly, but Scholander's pressure chamber of the 1960s made it possible to measure tension in plants.

Unit H6 Biodiversity

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
A3.2EvidenceIn 1977 Carl Woese used rRNA sequences to propose a third domain, Archaea. The idea met strong initial resistance.
A3.1Science as a shared endeavourDNA barcoding relies on shared international databases, where researchers across the world compare sequences.
A4.1ObservationsThe Grants observed a new finch lineage arise after a hybridization event on Daphne Major, within a few generations.
D4.1ModelsThe Hardy-Weinberg principle describes a population that does not really exist, yet it is useful for detecting when evolution is occurring.

Unit H7 Ecosystems

SubtopicAspectWhere it comes into play
D4.2HypothesesIn the early 1900s, Clements saw succession as ordered and predictable, while Gleason emphasised chance. Each view still shapes ecology.
D4.3Science as a shared endeavourPhenology uses records from scientists, the public and even centuries-old documents, such as Kyoto's cherry blossom dates.
