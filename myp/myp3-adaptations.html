Adaptations

How living things are suited to where they live, how energy flows through food chains, why plants lose water, how natural selection shapes species over time, and how humans are changing ecosystems.

MYP 3, Unit 214 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: EnvironmentRelated: EvidenceRelated: FormRelated: FunctionGlobal context: Orientation in space and time

Statement of inquiryThe relationships between organisms and their environment shape their form and function, and change over time.

Factual
What is an adaptation? How does energy pass along a food chain? What is natural selection?
Conceptual
How do structural, behavioural and functional adaptations help survival? How does a changing environment drive evolution?
Debatable
Should we bring extinct species back? Is it right to genetically modify organisms?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • define adaptation and give examples
  • classify adaptations as structural, behavioural or functional
  • distinguish herbivores, carnivores and omnivores
  • draw food chains and food webs and name trophic levels
  • describe how the polar bear and arctic animals survive the cold
  • explain why marsupials are found mainly in Australia
  • describe how desert plants save water
  • explain transpiration and the factors that change its rate
  • describe Darwin’s theory of natural selection
  • explain how fossils give evidence for evolution
  • compare natural and artificial selection and discuss genetic modification
  • describe how invasive species and humans cause extinction

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Adaptations Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
