Electrical safety and circuits

How electricity carries energy round a circuit, how we measure it, and how homes are wired to keep people safe. 15 lessons, ending with a test on what you know and understand (Criterion A).

MYP 5, Unit 315 lessonsMains in Vietnam: 220 V, 50 Hz

The big picture

Key concept: SystemsRelated: EnergyRelated: ConsequencesGlobal context: Scientific and technical innovation

Statement of inquiryUnderstanding how energy is carried round a circuit system lets us design and use devices that protect people from harm.

Factual
What are current, voltage and resistance, and how do we measure them? How do fuses, circuit breakers, earth wires and RCDs protect us?
Conceptual
Why is the current the same everywhere in a simple loop? Why can a tiny current through a person be deadly when a kettle safely takes about 10 A?
Debatable
Who is most responsible for electrical safety: the maker, the electrician or the user?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • explain why current is the same all round a loop
  • use Q = I × t, V = E ÷ Q and V = I × R
  • connect an ammeter in series and a voltmeter in parallel
  • draw circuit diagrams with the right symbols
  • describe the I–V graphs for a resistor and a lamp
  • explain how length, thickness, material and temperature change resistance
  • apply the series and parallel rules
  • use P = V × I, E = P × t and work out a cost in kWh
  • state what the live, neutral and earth wires do
  • calculate the current through a person and judge the danger
  • explain fuses, breakers, earth wires, double insulation and RCDs
  • choose a fuse rating

Revise in this order

  1. Work through the revision deck below: try each question before you click on.
  2. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and redo each worked example without looking.
  3. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then check it with your teacher.
  4. Practise in the labs: Circuit Practicals Lab and Home Safety Lab.
