Water potential

D2.3.8

What water potential is

p. 690

Water potential measures the stored energy held by a given volume of water, in kPa relative to pure water, which is set at 0 kPa. Solutes form hydrogen bonds with water molecules and lower their freedom to move, so solutions have negative water potentials.

D2.3.9

Direction of movement

p. 691

Water moves from higher (less negative) to lower (more negative) water potential, until the two are equal.

D2.3.10

Solute and pressure potential

pp. 691–692

Water potential = solute potential + pressure potential. Solute potential is zero or negative. Pressure potential is usually positive in cells with walls, as the protoplast pushes on the wall, but negative in xylem under tension.

D2.3.11

Water movement in plant tissue

p. 692

In a hypotonic solution, water enters: solute potential becomes less negative and pressure potential rises until the cell is turgid. In a hypertonic solution, water leaves: solute potential becomes more negative and pressure potential falls to zero, and the cell may plasmolyse.

Practical

Finding the water potential of a tissue

Lab50 minD2.3.9, D2.3.11

You will need: Potato cylinders, sucrose solutions from 0 to 1.0 M, balance, table of solute potentials

  1. Measure the percentage change in mass of cylinders in each solution
  2. Plot change in mass against concentration
  3. Read off the concentration where there is no change
  4. Convert this to water potential using the table, and explain the result

Safety: Take care with cork borers and knives. Wipe up spills.

Carry this out with your teacher's supervision and follow your school's lab rules.

Nature of science and TOK

Nature of science

Measurements

Water potential cannot be seen directly, but Scholander's pressure chamber of the 1960s made it possible to measure tension in plants.

Theory of knowledge

What does it mean to measure something abstract?

Water potential is a concept defined by a calculation. Consider whether it is a real property or a useful construct.
