Classifying Living Things

Why we sort things, how to tell living from non-living, how microscopes revealed a hidden world, how scientists grew the kingdoms from two to six, and how keys, the seven levels and two-word names help us identify every living thing.

MYP 1, Unit 212 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: SystemsRelated: PatternsRelated: DevelopmentRelated: EvidenceGlobal context: Orientation in space and time

Statement of inquiryOver time, scientists have used evidence and patterns to build systems that sort and name all living things.

Factual
What are the characteristics of living things? What are the six kingdoms? What are the seven levels of classification?
Conceptual
How do dichotomous keys help us identify organisms? How did new technology change the way we classify life?
Debatable
Is a virus alive? To what extent have two-word scientific names helped scientists around the world work together?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • explain why we sort things into groups
  • use the seven characteristics of life to decide if something is living
  • describe how van Leeuwenhoek and the microscope changed biology
  • name the parts of a microscope and use it to see cells
  • describe how classification grew from two kingdoms to six
  • describe protists and bacteria, useful and harmful
  • describe fungi and plan an investigation into mould growth
  • sort plants and animals into their main groups
  • use and build a dichotomous key
  • list the seven levels of classification in order
  • define species and write a scientific name correctly

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Sorting Life Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
