Molecules and Metals

How atoms share electrons, why metals bend and conduct, how carbon builds fuels and plastics, and how understanding bonding helps us use chemicals responsibly, at home and across the planet.

MYP 4, Unit 614 lessonsCriteria A, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: RelationshipsRelated: EnvironmentRelated: ConsequencesRelated: TransferGlobal context: Globalisation and sustainability

Statement of inquiryUnderstanding how substances are bonded helps us use chemicals responsibly, locally and globally.

Factual
How are covalent and metallic bonds formed? What is organic chemistry? How has the ozone layer been affected by human activity?
Conceptual
How can bonding and structure explain properties? Why is a homologous series a useful idea? Why do we need to take care with some chemicals?
Debatable
Is sorting substances into ionic, molecular and metallic too simple? Has our lifestyle become too dependent on chemicals?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • draw dot-and-cross diagrams and displayed formulas for simple molecules
  • explain the properties of simple molecular substances
  • compare diamond and graphite
  • describe metallic bonding and explain the properties of metals
  • explain why alloys are harder than pure metals
  • identify ionic, molecular, giant covalent and metallic structures from data
  • define hydrocarbon and describe crude oil
  • name alkanes, use CnH2n+2 and explain the boiling point trend
  • draw structural isomers
  • write and balance combustion equations and explain the danger of carbon monoxide
  • explain how CFCs damage the ozone layer and the Montreal Protocol
  • describe addition polymerisation and the bromine water test
  • read hazard pictograms and describe safe disposal

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Molecules Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
