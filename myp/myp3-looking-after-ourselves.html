Looking After Ourselves

How the body changes in adolescence, what a growing body needs, how bones, joints and muscles move us, why the teenage brain takes risks, and how alcohol, drugs, smoking and vaping affect our health now and in the future.

MYP 3, Unit 114 lessonsCriteria A, B, C and D

The big picture

Key concept: SystemsRelated: ConsequencesRelated: ModelsRelated: Form and functionRelated: Cause and effectGlobal context: Identities and relationships

Statement of inquiryLifestyle choices we make in adolescence can have physical, social and emotional health consequences, both immediately and in the future.

Factual
What happens to the body in puberty? How do bones, joints and muscles work together? How do the lungs exchange gases?
Conceptual
How do hormones control growing up? How do our choices affect our body systems? Why is the teenage brain more likely to take risks?
Debatable
Should energy drinks and vapes be banned for teenagers? Who is most responsible for a teenager’s health choices?

Revising for the test

Check you can…

  • distinguish between adolescence and puberty and describe the changes of puberty
  • explain how the pituitary gland and sex hormones control puberty, and outline the menstrual cycle
  • outline body image and its importance, and name nutrients and their jobs
  • describe the benefits of exercise and sleep, and explain why pulse rises during exercise
  • state the five functions of the skeleton and name important bones
  • compare types of joint and describe a synovial joint
  • explain how antagonistic muscle pairs move a joint
  • describe the developing teenage brain and explain risk-taking
  • outline peer pressure and refusal skills
  • describe how alcohol and drugs affect the adolescent body and brain
  • describe the path of air and how breathing works
  • explain gas exchange in the alveoli and plan an investigation into lung capacity
  • describe the effects of smoking and vaping on the body and on others

Revise in this order

  1. Read the unit notes, one section at a time, and learn the words in bold.
  2. Open each lesson and look through its slides, then redo Section C of its worksheet.
  3. Practise with the Health Lab: try the challenge at the bottom of each tab.
  4. Do the unit review sheet under test conditions, then try the sample Criterion A paper.
